Data Analysis
Q 01
Which topics are most popular?
All 4 topics each have exactly 40 Events, perfectly balanced by count. But by student interest, Web Dev and Generative AI attract far more people per event.

Q 02
Which events attract the highest attendance?
Contests dominate with an average of 104.9 student per event. Workshops follow at 87.0. Talks are a distant last at just 39.5, less than half of contests.
Contests: 104.9 avg
Q 03
Which events are rated the best?
Workshops lead with an average rating of 4.91, just ahed of contests at 4.87. Talks score noticeably lower at 4.20, suggesting they are less engaging, more boring.
Workships: 4.91 / 5
Q 04
Do harder topics get better or worse ratings?
Harder events tend to score lower. The Pearson correlation between difficulty and rating is -0.64, a moderately strong negative relationship. Hard ≠ impressive.
r = -0.64 (negative)
Q 05
Is there a pattern in difficulty vs rating?
Yes, a clear inverted-U pattern. Level 2 (easy) = 4.10. Level 3 (medium) = 4.91. Level 4 = 4.88. Level 5 (hardest) = 4.20. The sweet spot is difficulty level 3.
40 events each
Q 06
Do longer events attract more students?
Yes. Duration and attendance correlate at +0.69. Longer events bring more students — partly because Contests, the most attended type, also run longest at ~3 hours.
r = +0.69 (positive)

Web Development wins on raw attendance with 103.3 students per event on average. Generative AI is close second at 93.7. Quantum Computing trails significantly at 39.5.

Generative AI delivers 38.2 students per hour — the highest of any topic. This means it attracts a large, highly-engaged audience relative to the time invested. Quantum Computing delivers only 26.5 students per hour — a 44% gap. As the organizer, efficiency matters: your time and resources are limited.

Strategic Recommendations

5 Questions for the Organizer
01
Focus Next Semester On
Generative AI and Web Development.

Gen AI scores the highest in ratings (4.96) and efficiency (38.2/hr). Web Dev draws the biggest raw crowds (103.3 avg). Machine Learning is worth maintaining. Quantum compunting needs structural rework before it deserves more event slots.
02
Difficulty: Decrease or Increase?
Keep at Level 3. Lower Quantum Computing specifically.

The data is clear: Level 3 = peak satisfaction at 4.91. Level 5 drops to 4.20, identical to Level 2. Quantum computing runs 100% of its events at difficulty 5. That s the root cause of its poor numbers.
03
Best Event Blueprint
Generative AI · Workshop · 2.5 hrs · Difficulty 3.

This exact combination maximises rating, attendance, and student satisfaction. One event in the dataset matching this profile scored a perfect 5.0. It is the proven formula.
04
What Are We Doing Wrong?
Three clear problems.

1 — Too many Talks. They're the worst-rated and worst-attended format, yet appear throughout the year.

2 — Quantum Computing is permanently locked at max difficulty with no beginner entry point.

3 — No growth strategy. Attendance is flat with no campaigns or milestone events visible in the data.
05
One More Data Column to Collect
Registration count vs actual walk-ins.

Right now we only see who showed up. We have no idea how many registered and didn't come — which means we can't forecast demand, right-size venues, or identify whether our reminder communication is failing. One extra column unlocks all of that.

Generative AI Workshops at Difficulty 3. Thatis the Answer.

Everything else in this report is context. This is the conclusion.
